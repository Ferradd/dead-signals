The naive results looked incredible — which is exactly the trap. A whale-entry variant showed +352.4 SOL on ten trades. A leaderboard feature showed +73.2 SOL. Stop there and you have a strategy and a Twitter thread. Instead we ran four audits.

── audit Q1 · independent recount ───────────────────────────────

Fresh code re-derived every result from raw files — no shared functions, no shared assumptions. Sampled exit prices matched the real tape 10/10. The numbers didn't survive: n=10 is an anecdote, and in one group a single trade carried 42% of the entire EV.

── audit Q2 · point-in-time ────────────────────────────────────

For every feature: was it knowable at decision time? The leaderboards table stores fetch-time state — snapshot_date is NULL on every row. "Was this wallet a leaderboard wallet when it bought" silently became "is it one now". The alpha was the future leaking backward.

── audit Q3 · selection ────────────────────────────────────────

Surviving signals fired almost only in the top volume quintile — where the base win rate is already 23.7% vs 6.0% overall. Volume-matched controls collapsed the lift to ~1.24×. Two dev wallets accounted for ~12,500 SOL of aggregate P&L. A "population pattern" made of two actors.

── audit Q4 · execution ────────────────────────────────────────

Exits assumed zero price impact — infinite liquidity, the oldest lie in backtesting. Fixed with constant-product math + a 2% sandwich tax. The audit also caught a bare except swallowing a NameError on every time-stop exit: 539 of 1,618 simulated trades had silently vanished — selectively deleting exactly the ugly exits that drag EV down.

── the tape is hostile anyway ──────────────────────────────────

Over 50% of launches get sniped in block 0 (median first buy: 0 seconds). ~17% of all transactions are wash trades, and wash trading raises graduation odds up to 10× — so the label "graduated" is partly manufactured, and every feature trained on it inherits the manufacturing.
